Registration is three weeks of pain a year, which is why most clubs survive it rather than fix it.

That is a mistake, because registration is where the entire season's data quality gets decided. Every phone number typed wrong in August is a message that fails in November. Every waiver collected as a photo in a chat thread is a compliance question you cannot answer in March.

Here is the checklist, in the order that matters.

Before you open

1. Decide your fee structure and write it down

Not just the number — the whole thing. Registration fee, monthly dues, uniform, tournament entries. Which are mandatory, which are optional, what the sibling discount is, and what happens if a family joins in week six.

Write it out before you open, because you will be asked in week one, and improvising means two families on different terms for the same thing. That inconsistency is what makes a fee schedule impossible to enforce later.

2. Settle the payment-plan question now

Somebody is going to ask to split the fee. Decide in advance: do you offer a plan, over how many months, and does it cost more than paying up front?

A club with a written answer sounds professional. A club improvising sounds negotiable, and once one family negotiates, the rest find out.

3. Get the waivers right before anyone signs

Liability waiver, medical authorization, photo consent, code of conduct. Have the final wording ready and collect them digitally, attached to the player.

A signed PDF in someone's email is not a record. In March, when you need to prove a specific athlete was cleared on a specific date, you need it attached to that athlete, with a timestamp — not in a folder somebody has to search.

4. Make autopay the default path

This is the single highest-leverage decision in the whole checklist, and it takes one setting.

The families who pay on time are not more responsible than the ones who do not — they are the ones who set it up once and forgot. Offering autopay as an option gets you a minority. Making it the default path at signup, with opting out available, gets you most of the roster.

Every family that sets it up in August is a family you never chase between September and May.

While it is open

5. Let parents type their own information

Every field a volunteer transcribes from a paper form is a field that can be wrong, and it will be wrong in the phone number, which is the one that matters.

Parents typing their own contact details also means they own the error, which sounds petty until the third time you are blamed for a missed schedule change.

6. Ask for less than you think

Every extra field costs completions. You do not need the emergency contact's employer at signup. You need enough to take the money, reach the family and clear the player to play; everything else can be filled in later from the parent's own profile.

A long form on a phone at 10pm is where registrations get abandoned.

7. Watch the drop-off in the first 48 hours

Started-but-not-finished is your most valuable list in registration week. Those families decided to join and something stopped them — usually the form, the price, or a payment method that failed.

One message to that list recovers more registrations than any amount of promotion to people who never started.

After it closes

8. Reconcile before the first practice

Who registered but never paid. Who paid but never signed the waiver. Who signed up for the wrong age group. Every one of these is easy to fix in the week before the season and painful in the week after it starts.

The one that bites hardest: a player who is training but is not cleared to play. That is not an admin problem, it is an insurance problem.

9. Set the reminder cadence once

Decide when the dues reminder goes out — day 3 is the number that works — and set it once for the season, rather than deciding month by month whether this is the week you push.

An assistant that finds who is overdue, drafts the messages and shows you the list turns that from a decision into a ten-second approval. You approve; it executes.

The test

Two weeks into the season, ask yourself: can I answer "is this player cleared and paid up?" in under ten seconds, for any kid in the program?

If yes, registration went well. If it takes opening three things, next August is where you fix it — not next March.
